A panel and an electrical service are different parts of the system

The panel distributes electricity to property circuits. The service is the incoming supply and associated equipment. Replacing a panel does not automatically increase the available supply. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess existing condition and demand, planned loads and the utility requirements before recommending equipment. Spare breaker spaces alone do not establish capacity.

Prepare the scope for your Grimsby property

List how each unit is used, which meters you can see from outside and where each unit's panel door is. Photograph each closed panel door and any labels on its outside without opening anything, and note any equipment the units share.

Ask the contractor whether the units can be fed separately, whether the utility must be involved, and what written description of the supply they can provide afterward.

What should the assessment establish?

A second living unit in a Grimsby house is to have its supply separated. How each unit is fed today is established on site and recorded, together with whether that arrangement can be described accurately in writing. Splitting the supply is different work from adding breaker positions, and the contractor treats the two as separate questions. The written scope names who must be notified, including the utility and ESA, and says whether a building permit is also needed. If the home is being readied for sale, a buyer may raise the same supply question.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a second unit always need its own electrical supply?

Not always. The answer depends on how the units are fed and on what the insurer, lender or municipality requires. Ask the contractor to describe the present arrangement and the options in writing before any change is priced.

Who should I tell before changing the supply to a second unit?

Confirm first with the insurer, the lender or both, since a change may alter what they ask about. The contractor then explains what the utility, ESA and the municipality need, and who must apply for each.
