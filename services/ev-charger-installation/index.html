EV charger installation in Grimsby.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Grimsby property

Photograph the wall or post location and the parking position from a few steps back, and describe in words the route between the charger and the panel. Collect the charger instructions and note how exposed that spot is to rain and splashing.

Ask the contractor whether the proposed equipment is approved and outdoor-rated, how the route to the panel will be protected, and which document will describe the finished installation.

What should the assessment establish?

Whether the supply can serve a charger on an exterior wall or driveway post in Grimsby, or how its load will be managed instead, is for the contractor to confirm in writing. The same contractor names the equipment, its suitability for outdoor use and the dedicated circuit it will run on, and records the route, the mounting method and the protection from vehicle contact. If you are putting the home on the market, hold on to a plain summary of the unit for a buyer's inspector. Whether ESA needs a notification, and who files it, is stated in the scope.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Will an insurer want to know about a charger on my driveway post?

It may, since the equipment is outdoors and adds a new load. Ask the insurer what it requires, then keep the equipment details and the installation record together so you can answer accurately.

Which papers prove what was mounted outdoors for the charger?

Keep the equipment instructions, the contractor's written scope, the invoice and any ESA notification paperwork the contractor provides. Photograph the finished unit and its position so the record shows what exists.

Useful project details

  • Vehicle and charger model if chosen
  • Parking position and preferred equipment location
  • Indoor or outdoor conditions
  • Existing supply records if available
  • Other major loads and future charging needs

Only photograph labels and locations that you can see safely. Do not remove electrical covers or approach damaged equipment to gather information.
